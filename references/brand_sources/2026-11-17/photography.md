## Page text

Photography should capture the real, unpolished moments of life at RPI, focusing on people collaborating, experimenting, and using equipment and facilities.

Images should showcase research labs, classrooms, and life across campus, including EMPAC, CBIS, and other key locations. Favor candid, process-oriented shots over staged portraits.

Look for the breakthroughs, the hands-on problem solving, and the organic interactions that happen when diverse minds work together. Show the journey of discovery, not just polished outcomes. Capture the building, testing, breaking, and rebuilding that reflects RPI's academic culture.

Key themes include: community, experimentation, authenticity, and discovery.

Campus life

Labs, classrooms, common spaces: Capture students and faculty mid-experiment, mid-discovery. Show the moment of testing, building, breaking, and rebuilding. Focus on candids of students and faculty actively working with materials, tools, and technology.

Campus environments: Elevated shots, most likely outdoors, that show the interconnected nature of campus activity. People moving between disciplines, collaborating across different spaces, the organic flow of a community solving problems together.

Dynamic spaces: Capture spaces being used in unexpected ways that reflect the experimental and innovative RPI mindset.

Community and collaboration

Academic research and classroom environments: Show diverse minds working together on complex problems. Capture cross-disciplinary partnerships, if possible.

Student union clubs and campus life: Show how creativity and innovation extend beyond formal academics into student life.

Architecture and region

Signature buildings in action: Photograph EMPAC, CBIS, and other spaces with activity, showing how distinctive architecture supports distinctive work. Avoid empty spaces.

Local and regional context: Show RPI's place in Troy and the broader region (where possible), reflecting the partnership values and community connections that extend beyond campus. Avoid empty spaces.

Details and process

Works in progress: Focus on the experiments, prototypes, and tangible evidence of "what if we tried this?" Capture the tools and materials that bring ideas to reality.

Precision and craft: Capture the precision and, literally, focus required for innovation. Make every effort to avoid clichéd compositions.

Humans making: The setup, the cleanup, the iteration process. Document the unglamorous but essential work behind progress.

PHOTO + TYPE

When applying type on photography, always consider the photo first. Is the type legible? Is there space for type without covering key parts of the image?

Type should always be placed in a corner, instead of floating.

When the image is busy, don't apply partial effects like gradients or blurs. Instead, reduce the photo's opacity over a black background. You can also use a solid color background.

Type placed on photography should always be black or white depending on legibility, but don't adjust a photo's opacity on a white background: it should always be placed on a black background to avoid washing out the photo.
