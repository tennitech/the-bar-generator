## Page text

Our color system is built on institutional heritage while prioritizing clarity, accessibility, and purposeful application. The palette draws from RPI’s historic 1904 seal, establishing a foundation that honors our past and supporting modern communication needs.

PRIMARY COLORS

Our primary palette establishes RPI’s visual foundation with black, white, and red as the institutional core. This restrained approach ensures maximum impact when color is applied purposefully.

The Role of Red

Red is one of RPI’s most powerful brand assets — and our most easily misused. When applied thoughtfully, red creates energy, commands attention, and reinforces institutional pride. When overused, it overwhelms content and diminishes user experience.

Red should energize, not dominate. When in doubt, default to black and white.

When Red Works

Activation moments: Calls-to-action, interactive elements, navigation highlights

Athletic events: “Big Red Freakout” and spirit applications where energy is the goal

Ceremonial occasions: Formal events, Commencement, and institutional celebrations

Accent applications: Highlighting key information or creating visual emphasis

When to Reduce Red

Content-focused layouts: When photography, student work, or complex information is primary

Large background areas: Red backgrounds can strain readability and accessibility

Interface-heavy applications: Where user experience requires visual calm

Text applications: Except for very specific emphasis or links

SECONDARY COLORS

Our secondary palette introduces blue and gold — the primary colors that form the foundation of all color creation. These accent colors, from which we also derive our silver and gray, provide flexibility while maintaining institutional consistency.

COLOR ACCESSIBILITY

EXAMPLES

Secondary Color Usage

Bold brand colors (RPI red, blue, and gold) should generally be used as background color fills. Don't mix these bold brand colors together in a single composition (poster, Instagram story, etc.). For a series, you can use different bold colors across individual compositions.

Use the neutral colors (gray, silver) to ground compositions and allow bolder brand colors to be used for emphasis.

Typography should generally be black and white for legibility, but bolder colors can be applied to small type and to create typographic texture in the background.

Don't allow the secondary palette to outshine the primary brand colors. Across RPI communications, use red as the dominant brand color, with blue and gold as supporting colors. While red should be the most frequently used and prominently featured brand color in the totality of RPI's visual presence, individual compositions can certainly use blue or gold without red.

For digital applications, these color combinations have been tested to meet WCAG AA standards, with many exceeding AAA requirements. Only use accessible combinations to ensure text readability for all users.

Color Accessibility Checklist

Use accessible color combinations only.

Maintain WCAG AA contrast minimums (4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text).

Don't use color alone to convey information,

Test combinations with accessibility tools when creating new applications.
