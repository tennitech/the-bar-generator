## Page text

RPI’s pattern system extends the measurement bar’s creative logic into a flexible visual language that can scale across any application — from social media graphics to environmental installations. These patterns encode meaning through systematic approaches while maintaining the technical precision that defines our identity.

PHILOSOPHY

Patterns are the bar’s logic liberated from constraints — able to flow across any format, any scale, any surface. They carry encoded information through systematic approaches: mathematical sequences, data visualizations, algorithmic generation, or purposeful randomization.

Each pattern tells a story through structure, whether it’s the golden ratio governing architectural proportions, Fibonacci sequences found in nature, or data streams visualizing campus activity. The logic is embedded, the meaning is discoverable.

CREATIVE APPROACHES

Patterns draw from the same creative territories as the measurement bar system: mathematical and systematic logic, data visualization, algorithmic generation, and responsive systems. These approaches can be applied at any scale or format to create meaningful visual expressions. See The Bar page for detailed creative territories and examples.

See The Bar Guidelines

USAGE GUIDELINES

Flexible Applications

Fill any shape: circles, rectangles, irregular forms, or complete canvases.

Serve as masks for photography and video content.

Layer over images to create depth and meaning.

Animate in motion graphics and digital displays.

Scale from thumbnail to building-scale installations.

Encode with Purpose

Root every pattern in measurable logic: mathematical, scientific, or data-driven.

Document the underlying system — patterns should teach as well as decorate.

Consider viewing distance and context in pattern density.

Reinforce RPI’s values: precision, innovation, and technical excellence.

Visual Consistency

Use only approved brand colors.

Employ only primitive geometries: rectangles, circles, lines, polygons.

Ensure scalability across micro and macro applications.

ACCESSIBILITY

Be aware that some patterns can create moiré effects that may trigger seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy (approximately 3% of people with epilepsy). These effects are not a design defect. They are a well-studied mathematical and visual phenomenon that artists and designers often deliberately pursue for their striking impact. While RPI’s patterns are designed to add visual impact, they should be applied mindfully by considering the audience and viewing context.

Safety Considerations

At small scales, screen display may increase moiré effects

Consider using color combinations with reduced contrast

Animation may amplify visual effects

Overlapping patterns increase visual interference

DON’TS

EXAMPLES

Patterns transform RPI’s spaces and communications into dynamic canvases that reflect our commitment to systematic thinking, creative problem-solving, and the beauty found in technical precision.

Don’t create patterns without underlying systematic logic.

Don’t use photography, gradients, or complex imagery as pattern elements.

Don’t compromise accessibility or readability for aesthetic effect.

Don’t ignore scalability requirements across different applications.
