## Page text

RPI’s voice embodies two centuries of experimental spirit while remaining grounded and approachable. Our voice is distinct — shaped by our collaborative culture and commitment to solving complex problems at the intersections of disciplines.

USING THESE GUIDELINES

These guidelines define RPI's institutional voice — not the voice of any individual member of the community. When you write on behalf of RPI, you're channeling the voice of the institution itself. This means setting aside your personal communication style to represent RPI's collective identity and values.

These are guidelines, not templates. When you see examples throughout these guidelines, they're fictional illustrations designed to demonstrate the principles in action. Use them to understand the thinking behind effective RPI communications, then apply that same thinking to your unique content and context.

Adapt the tone while staying true to the voice. RPI's core voice traits (noted below) remain constant, but how you express them should flex based on your audience, medium, and purpose. A social media post celebrating a breakthrough will sound different from a technical research summary, but both should feel authentically RPI.

When in doubt, refer back to the core question: "Does this sound like the kind of organization we are?" These guidelines exist to help you communicate with consistency and authenticity as the institution, not to constrain your creativity or force artificial language.

OUR VOICE

Curious

We approach every topic with genuine interest and inquiry. We ask "What if we tried this?" and invite others to explore alongside us.

Humble

We're confident in our expertise but modest in our presentation. We let our work speak for itself rather than making grand proclamations.

Genuine

We communicate with authenticity and sincerity. Our enthusiasm for discovery and innovation comes through naturally, not through manufactured excitement.

Resilient

We demonstrate strength and adaptability when facing challenges. We frame setbacks as learning opportunities and obstacles as problems waiting for creative solutions.

Offbeat

We embrace our uniqueness and aren't afraid to show personality. We celebrate the unexpected connections and surprising solutions that emerge from our collaborative environment.

Relatable

We maintain a down-to-earth appeal that makes complex ideas accessible. We're the approachable experts, not the ivory tower academics.

Our voice should never be

Obvious: We avoid stating the expected or mundane. Instead of saying what everyone knows, we reveal what others haven't considered.

Arrogant: We reject superiority or condescension in all forms. We share knowledge to build understanding, not to demonstrate intellectual dominance.

Pretentious: We steer clear of urban elitism or academic snobbery. Our sophistication comes through substance, not style.

Hyperbolic: We avoid over-the-top promotional language. We let the genuine excitement of discovery drive our enthusiasm rather than manufactured marketing energy.

Overall

Ground all communications in evidence: Whether technical or accessible, avoid empty claims.

Show the journey, not just the destination: Acknowledge the process, iterations, and collaborative effort.

Connect to human purpose: Always tie back to how work serves people and communities.

Own achievements without arrogance: Balance deserved pride with institutional humility.

Replace superlatives with specifics: Use concrete examples rather than generic excellence language.

EXAMPLES

This is sample text written solely to illustrate tone, style, and structure. Any institutions, people, or programs referenced are entirely fictional.

Informal

Academic Program Description

Here you'll debug climate models with environmental scientists, build AI that helps doctors interpret medical images, and create interfaces that make complex systems feel intuitive.

Our curriculum creates space for experimentation across disciplines. The technical foundation is rigorous — you'll master everything from theoretical computer science to distributed systems — but the applications stretch into territories that surprise even us. Because the most interesting code doesn't just solve problems; it reveals even bigger questions.

Voice qualities: Curious, genuine (specific applications), humble ("surprise even us"). Tone variations: future-facing, accessible, specific, human-technical balance, slightly informal.

Research Spotlight

When Professor Smith’s underwater robot kept getting tangled in lake weeds, her team didn't see failure — they saw a design challenge. “What if we stopped fighting the environment and started learning from it?” she wondered.

Six months later, their bio-inspired robot moves through underwater vegetation like a fish, collecting water quality data that was previously impossible to gather. The breakthrough came from an unexpected collaboration: biology students who understood how eels navigate, engineering students who could translate that into mechanical systems, and computer science students who programmed adaptive movement algorithms.

Voice qualities: Resilient (reframing failure), curious (“What if...”), genuine (real challenges and solutions). Tone variations: human-leaning, simple over complex.

Alumni Stories

Maya Polytechnic never planned to revolutionize prosthetics design. But when she partnered with local physical therapy clinics during her junior year, she noticed something: traditional prosthetics focused on function but ignored the sensory feedback that makes movement feel natural.

"At RPI, you learn to see connections others miss," Maya explains. Her background in biomedical engineering, combined with coursework in cognitive science and materials research, led her to develop prosthetics that provide haptic feedback to users. Today, her startup is helping amputees regain not just mobility, but the feeling of touch. What started as a student wondering "What if prosthetics could feel?" has become technology that’s reshaping rehabilitation medicine.

Voice qualities: Genuine (authentic story progression), curious (“What if...”), humble (no boastful claims). Tone variations: authentic, future-facing.

Social Posts

When your 3D printer breaks, you don’t buy a new one — you redesign the broken part and make it better. Peak RPI energy right there.

Plot twist: the robotics club’s prototype escaped the lab and is now delivering snacks around campus. It wasn’t programmed to do that...it just evolved?! We’re equally proud and terrified.

Last night’s show in the Union was absolutely unreal. John Guitarman had a packed house wrapped around every riff — the vibes are strong with that one.

Voice qualities: Quirky, genuine, relatable. Tone variations: informal, specific, accessible.

Formal

Convocation Ceremony Program

Today, we gather not just to mark another beginning, but to celebrate the spirit of inquiry that has driven RPI forward for more than two centuries. As you embark on your academic journey, you join a community united by a simple yet powerful question: “What if we tried this?”

The challenges facing our world — from climate change and health care equity to energy systems and the ethics of AI — demand the kind of creative problem-solving that happens when brilliant minds collaborate across traditional boundaries. Your education here will prepare you not just with technical expertise, but with the resilience to tackle problems that don't yet have names.

We invite you to approach your studies with curiosity, to embrace the productive friction of interdisciplinary thinking, and to remember that the most meaningful innovations often emerge from unexpected collaborations. Welcome to a place where engineering meets artistry, where rigorous analysis pairs with creative experimentation, and where your unique perspective contributes to our collective pursuit of knowledge.

Voice qualities: Humble (acknowledging challenges), curious (celebrating inquiry), resilient (preparing for unknown problems). Tone variations: formal but accessible, future-facing, authoritative balanced with humble.

Grant Award Press Release

A collaborative team led by RPI's Department of Materials Science and Engineering has been awarded a three-year, $3.2 million grant from the National Science Foundation to develop bio-inspired materials that could transform renewable energy storage.

The project brings together researchers from materials science, biology, and computer modeling to investigate how natural structures achieve remarkable strength and efficiency. “Nature has been experimenting with advanced materials for millions of years,” said Sarah Smith, Ph.D. “We’re curious about what we can learn from those experiments.”

The interdisciplinary approach reflects RPI’s commitment to tackling complex challenges through collaborative research. Graduate students will work alongside faculty from multiple departments, while undergraduate researchers contribute to computational modeling and materials testing. Industry partners from the renewable energy sector will provide real-world applications for the laboratory discoveries.

This funding builds on RPI’s growing reputation in sustainable technology research and positions the Institute to address critical challenges in energy storage — work that could have significant implications for renewable energy adoption worldwide.

Voice qualities: Genuine (specific details and real quotes), humble (learning from nature), curious (exploring natural experiments). Tone variations: authoritative, specific, professional.
