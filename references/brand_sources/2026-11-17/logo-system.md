## Page text

Our logo embodies RPI’s commitment to precision, experimentation, and setting new standards. This isn’t just another logo — it’s a flexible identity system that encodes the multitude of dimensions, domains, and real-world contexts of RPI’s research and education impact.

PRIMARY LOGO

The primary RPI logo consists of our custom letterforms anchored by the bar — a variable element derived from the surveyor’s rod in the original 1904 Institute seal — which provides endless creative opportunities for contemporary expression.

The bar serves as both a visual anchor and generative canvas, capable of containing embedded meaning such as data visualizations, technical metrics, encoded language, or algorithmic patterns that reflect RPI’s diverse academic and research pursuits.

All variable versions of the primary logo shown can be used interchangeably unless they are designated for specific purposes. Example usage includes:

Default application for all external communications

Marketing materials and brand touchpoints

Digital platforms and social media

Official correspondence and documentation

See The Bar Guidelines

Primary Logo Composition

The primary RPI logo was carefully redrawn from the Replica typeface.

The height of the bar matches the width of the lettering, and the spacing between letters matches the space between the letters and the bar.

The letterforms and bar must maintain fixed proportional relationships with no modifications to the spacing, sizing, or positioning of elements.

LOCKUPS

What is a Logo Lockup?

A lockup is a standardized logo format designed to represent major, permanent university entities with distinct public-facing missions. These lockups maintain visual consistency while establishing clear institutional hierarchy.

Our lockup system creates clear hierarchy while maintaining brand consistency across RPI’s diverse academic, research, and administrative units. All lockups are designed to work at large, medium, and micro scales depending on the space available.

Structural Consistency

Fixed spacing and proportional relationships across all lockups

Standardized format accommodates both short and longer organizational names

Measurement bar maintains consistent visual relationship to RPI letterforms

Specialized Formats

Additional lockup formats are available for specific spatial contexts including environmental signage, hardware applications, and constrained layouts where the standard format won’t fit.

Who Receives an Official Lockup?

Schools, academic departments, major administrative offices, and institute-led research centers

Who Does Not Receive a Lockup?

Academic programs, temporary initiatives and campaigns, student organizations, or projects. These entities use the primary RPI logo separately alongside their name in approved typography.

For questions about lockup eligibility, please submit a ticket using the marketing and communications form found on the "Contact Us" page on the left-hand navigation bar.

Primary Logo Placement

Preferred positioning in upper-left or lower-left corners, but any layout corner works for the primary logo.

Lockup Placement

Standard Institute lockups work best positioned in upper-left or lower-left corners of a layout.

Separate Elements

The primary logo and entity name may also be used as separate elements — not in a lockup — to act as a frame placed on a layout grid.

Partnership branding occurs when RPI is involved with an organization or initiative — it is a participant, partner, or member — but is not the sole operator. There are two ways to display partnerships that depend on the nature of the relationship.

1. Equal Partnership

Use case: RPI partners directly with one other institution on equal terms, which may include co-ownership of centers, programs, or initiatives.

Example: The Center for Engineering and Precision Medicine, which RPI and the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai operate jointly, sharing governance and resources

Logo: Use primary RPI logo with minimum size and clear space requirements.

Positioning: Partner logos shall be positioned side-by-side with equal visual prominence. Neither logo may be enlarged, emphasized, or positioned in a manner that suggests hierarchical dominance.

Size: All participating institutional logos must maintain similar optical sizing. This means visual weight equivalence rather than mathematical measurements, as logos vary in density and composition.

Spacing: Maintain a minimum clear space equal between partner logos, ensuring visual separation while maintaining cohesive grouping. Use the height of the letter “R” to guide logo separation and divider placement. Use the width of “RPI” to guide the height of the divider separating the marks, using a stroke no thinner than 1px.

Alignment: Align logos to vertical center.

2. Multi-Institution Membership

Use case: RPI joins a group of multiple institutions under one umbrella organization or initiative.

Example: The New York Fashion Innovation Center, where RPI is one of several participating institutions. The Fashion Innovation Center's logo appears prominently, with RPI’s logo alongside other member institutions in equal sizing.

Positioning: Main organization's logo appears prominently, all member logos (including RPI) appear smaller.

Spacing: Maintain a minimum clear space equal between partner logos, ensuring visual separation while maintaining cohesive grouping.

Alignment: All logos must align on a consistent baseline or be center-aligned vertically when logos vary in height proportions.

Clear Space

Digital: minimum of 10px around all sides of logo

Print: minimum of 0.25 inch around all sides of logo

Minimum Sizes

Digital: 60px width with solid bar

Print: 0.75 inch width with solid bar

Color Combinations

Primary use: Black on white, white on black, and white on red

Limited use: Red on white, red on black, and secondary palette combinations that maintain accessible contrast

Don’t re-create or redraw the logo elements.

Don’t change the proportions or spacing between elements.

Don’t stretch or distort any part of the logo.

Don’t add outlines, shadows, or visual effects.

Don’t compromise legibility or accessibility standards.

Don’t use colors outside the approved palette.

The logo’s power lies in its consistent application. Any modifications undermine brand recognition and institutional authority.
